Security Operations Center (SOC) Fundamentals

A Security Operations Center, or SOC, is the team and facility responsible for
continuously monitoring an organization's networks, endpoints, and applications
for signs of malicious activity. A SOC typically operates in tiers. Tier 1
analysts perform initial triage of alerts generated by tools such as SIEM
platforms, EDR agents, and IDS/IPS sensors. Tier 2 analysts investigate
escalated alerts in more depth, correlating logs across multiple sources to
confirm whether an incident is a true positive. Tier 3 analysts, often called
threat hunters, proactively search for hidden threats that automated tools
missed, using hypotheses grounded in frameworks like MITRE ATT&CK.

SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) platforms such as Splunk,
IBM QRadar, and Microsoft Sentinel are the backbone of most SOCs. They collect
log data from firewalls, servers, cloud services, and endpoints, normalize
that data into a common format, and apply correlation rules to detect
suspicious patterns. A well-tuned SIEM reduces alert fatigue by filtering out
noise and surfacing only alerts that indicate genuine risk.

Key SOC metrics include Mean Time to Detect (MTTD) and Mean Time to Respond
(MTTR). Lower values for both indicate a more mature and efficient security
operation. Many SOCs also track the ratio of true positives to false
positives to evaluate how well their detection rules are tuned.

A typical incident response workflow followed by SOC teams has six phases:
preparation, identification, containment, eradication, recovery, and lessons
learned. During containment, analysts isolate affected systems to prevent
lateral movement. During eradication, the root cause -- such as malware or a
compromised credential -- is removed. The lessons-learned phase feeds back
into detection rule tuning and staff training.
